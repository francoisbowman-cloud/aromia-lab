## Batch visual thesis

This batch must feel like nine different editorial investigations seen through one publication, not nine campaign pages using the same photographic recipe.

Shared rules:

- Aromia identity comes from the canonical shell, typography, spacing discipline and material tokens; individual stories may vary composition.
- No generic AI-luxury perfume imagery, no invented branded bottles, no floating ingredients around a centered flacon, no fake magazine mockups.
- Product photography is not the default. Several pieces should contain no hero perfume bottle at all.
- Human scenes must look observed rather than directed: ordinary rooms, believable gestures, imperfect objects, incidental light, no luxury-showroom staging unless the story itself demands it.
- Material studies should explain through juxtaposition, scale, repetition, surface or spatial relationships rather than infographic conventions.
- The 18 opportunity markers are not 18 mandatory generated photographs. Some become paired compositions, documentary fragments or typography/material fields.
- Original photographic generation must obey `AROMIA_VISUAL_GENERATION_ISOLATION_PROTOCOL.md`; the operational turn prepares clean capsules but must not generate from repo/QA context.
- Documentary material must be authentic, licensed/public-domain as appropriate, and captioned/provenanced.

## Batch rhythm

Opening visuals across the nine pieces deliberately rotate among:

1. observed human photography;
2. object/document study;
3. material still life;
4. environmental scene;
5. editorial juxtaposition.

Second visual moments should generally change visual grammar from the opening rather than repeat it.

# 01 — Antes del perfume ya olíamos

## Direction

Treat the body as inhabited territory, not beauty surface and not hygiene problem. The piece should feel physically close, quiet and matter-of-fact.

### Opportunity A — opening

**Decision:** ORIGINAL EDITORIAL PHOTOGRAPHY.

**Concept:** An ordinary person immediately after a shower, but without the visual language of skincare advertising. Cropped to shoulder, upper back, neck and damp hairline; towel edge, bathroom wall joint and slight condensation may remain. No face required. Skin texture visible. The image is about the fact that a body already has presence before any bottle appears.

**Composition:** Tall image entering from the reading field with generous paper around it; not full-bleed. Slightly off-center crop so the body feels observed rather than posed.

**Narrative test:** `A body exists here before perfume enters the story.`

**Clean generation capsule:**
SUBJECT / SCENE: ordinary adult just after shower; shoulder, upper back, neck, damp hairline
ENVIRONMENT: modest real bathroom, neutral domestic finishes
COMPOSITION: close crop, slightly lateral, imperfect framing
CAMERA / POINT OF VIEW: eye-level close observational photograph
LIGHT: soft natural bathroom/window light, no glamour rim light
MATERIAL / TEXTURE: real skin, damp hair, towel cotton, faint condensation
HUMAN IMPERFECTIONS / LIVED-IN DETAILS: natural skin texture, towel edge, wall seam
PALETTE: warm-neutral skin against mineral/quiet bathroom surfaces
AUTHENTICITY CONSTRAINTS: ordinary human body; no aspirational beauty retouching
MUST NOT APPEAR: perfume, cosmetics display, spa props, logos, text, UI, diagrams, mockups
CROP / RESPONSIVE INTENT: portrait master with safe center-left crop for mobile
NARRATIVE TEST: a body already exists before perfume

### Opportunity B — pre-final reflection

**Decision:** NON-LITERAL MATERIAL COMPOSITION, preferably CSS + photographic texture rather than another human photo.

**Concept:** A close field of translucent moisture/skin-like mineral texture crossed by a second thin veil entering from one edge. The visual metaphor is intervention over a process already in motion, not perfume “creating” identity.

**Implementation intent:** Use existing material tokens; no private palette. If photographic texture is needed, generate only a physical macro surface, not conceptual graphics.

**Narrative test:** `Something enters an existing field; it does not replace it.`

# 02 — Comprar para oler o comprar para tener

## Direction

The visual argument is possession versus experience. Avoid moral hierarchy. Objects should look personally owned.

### Opportunity A — opening

**Decision:** ORIGINAL OBJECT PHOTOGRAPHY.

**Concept:** A tiny anonymous sample vial with visible use beside a full-size generic fragrance bottle that is nearly untouched, both on an ordinary dresser or shelf. The vial is physically smaller but closer to camera; the bottle sits farther back and occupies more space. Neither is hero-lit.

**Composition:** Horizontal still life with unequal distances. Let the sample carry evidence of handling: imperfect label without readable brand, small scuff, cap slightly misaligned.

**Narrative test:** `One object contains the experience; the other contains ownership.`

**Clean generation capsule:**
SUBJECT / SCENE: used anonymous perfume sample vial beside nearly-full generic perfume bottle
ENVIRONMENT: ordinary bedroom dresser or domestic shelf
COMPOSITION: sample closer to camera, larger bottle farther back; asymmetric spacing
CAMERA / POINT OF VIEW: shelf-height, slightly oblique
LIGHT: soft side daylight, no glossy advertising setup
MATERIAL / TEXTURE: glass, paper label, worn wood/laminate, subtle dust
HUMAN IMPERFECTIONS / LIVED-IN DETAILS: scuffed vial, slightly crooked label, everyday surface
PALETTE: quiet domestic neutrals
AUTHENTICITY CONSTRAINTS: generic unbranded objects; believable scale
MUST NOT APPEAR: luxury retail staging, recognizable perfume geometry, logos, text overlays, UI, mockups
CROP / RESPONSIVE INTENT: landscape master; objects remain separable on mobile crop
NARRATIVE TEST: experience and possession are different desires

### Opportunity B — collection section

**Decision:** DIPTYCH OF TWO LIVED-IN SHELVES.

**Concept:** Left: several bottles at visibly different liquid levels, fingerprints and everyday disorder. Right: several nearly-full bottles aligned more carefully, still not retail-perfect. Do not label one “good” and one “bad.”

**Composition:** Two equal-width documentary frames separated by paper, not a comparison card.

**Narrative test:** `Both shelves are legitimate; they reveal different relationships to the same objects.`

# 03 — Cuando tú ya no hueles tu perfume pero todos los demás sí

## Direction

This piece can be the most human and lightly comic of the batch. The visual language is small social miscalibration, not neuroscience illustration.

### Opportunity A — opening

**Decision:** ORIGINAL OBSERVATIONAL HUMAN PHOTOGRAPHY.

**Concept:** A person bringing a wrist toward the nose in a normal room, eyes slightly uncertain, other hand hovering near but not yet reaching for a bottle placed out of focus at the edge. The moment before reapplication.

**Composition:** Medium crop, natural gesture, more empty room than beauty campaign.

**Narrative test:** `I cannot tell whether it is still there.`

**Clean generation capsule:**
SUBJECT / SCENE: adult casually smelling own wrist, uncertain whether perfume remains
ENVIRONMENT: ordinary bedroom or hallway at home
COMPOSITION: medium candid frame; bottle only peripheral and blurred if present
CAMERA / POINT OF VIEW: nearby observer, eye-level
LIGHT: ordinary window/room light
MATERIAL / TEXTURE: real clothing, skin, domestic surfaces
HUMAN IMPERFECTIONS / LIVED-IN DETAILS: relaxed posture, slight clutter, natural expression
PALETTE: neutral everyday interior
AUTHENTICITY CONSTRAINTS: candid gesture, not perfume-ad pose
MUST NOT APPEAR: smell waves, diagrams, lab imagery, product hero, logos, text, UI, mockups
CROP / RESPONSIVE INTENT: 4:5 master adaptable to mobile vertical
NARRATIVE TEST: uncertainty immediately before needless reapplication

### Opportunity B — asymmetry section

**Decision:** SINGLE SOCIAL SCENE, not infographic.

**Concept:** In a small elevator, corridor or shared room, the wearer is relaxed and unaware while another person nearby subtly registers the scent—small head turn or raised eyebrow, not theatrical disgust. The wearer does not notice.

**Narrative test:** `The same scent is background to one person and new information to another.`

# 04 — Fougère no significa viejo

**Composition:** Environmental detail rather than wide establishing shot. Let material age carry the association.

**Narrative test:** `This is the memory people are calling “old,” not the molecular structure itself.`

**Composition:** Asymmetric overhead/oblique material study with generous negative space and small documentary caption in implementation.

**Narrative test:** `Craft continues by rebuilding its vocabulary under constraint.`

**Composition:** Restrained near-symmetry intentionally broken by tiny imperfections.

**Narrative test:** `You cannot diagnose molecular origin by looking—and often not simply by smelling either.`

COMPOSITION: near-symmetrical pair with subtle natural imperfections

# 08 — ¿Podemos describir un olor sin compararlo con otra cosa?

## Direction

This should be the most conceptual piece visually, but not infographic. The page should make language feel insufficient through sensory juxtaposition.

### Opportunity A — opening

**Decision:** EDITORIAL OBJECT / MATERIAL COMPOSITION.

**Concept:** Three physical odor sources—a torn citrus peel, damp piece of wood, clean paper or fabric—partially cropped so they are recognized by texture before being named. No words in the image. The visual itself forces the same comparison mechanism the article discusses.

**Composition:** Large negative field with objects entering from different edges, not a flat-lay catalog.

**Narrative test:** `Before language arrives, the reader already names the smell by naming the thing.`

**Clean generation capsule:**
SUBJECT / SCENE: partial close views of citrus peel, damp wood, clean paper/fabric on one physical surface
ENVIRONMENT: simple editorial table
COMPOSITION: objects enter from edges with large negative space; not a grid
CAMERA / POINT OF VIEW: close oblique/overhead hybrid
LIGHT: directional soft daylight emphasizing texture
MATERIAL / TEXTURE: oily citrus skin, damp wood grain, dry paper/fabric fiber
HUMAN IMPERFECTIONS / LIVED-IN DETAILS: torn peel, irregular damp edge, folded paper
PALETTE: natural citrus/wood/paper restrained against Aromia paper field
AUTHENTICITY CONSTRAINTS: real recognizable materials, no symbolic surrealism
MUST NOT APPEAR: written words, typography inside image, dictionaries, diagrams, speech bubbles, UI, mockups
CROP / RESPONSIVE INTENT: landscape master with independent object survival on mobile crop
NARRATIVE TEST: naming the source becomes the default way to name the smell

### Opportunity B — Jahai section

**Decision:** DOCUMENTARY / TYPOGRAPHIC RESEARCH MOMENT, NOT GENERATIVE CULTURAL IMAGERY.

Do not fabricate an “indigenous language” scene. Use properly sourced documentary/academic material if rights allow, or an editorial typographic treatment of three verified Jahai odor terms from the cited research with clear source attribution. This is the one piece in the batch where typography is itself legitimate subject matter because the story is explicitly about vocabulary.

**Narrative test:** `Another language already has compact words where ours reaches for comparisons.`

# 09 — Por qué una lista de notas no te dice cómo huele un perfume

## Direction

The visual system should move from familiar note-list artifact to the physical logic of an accord. Avoid replicating brand pyramids.

### Opportunity A — opening

**Decision:** AUTHORED DOCUMENT STUDY.

**Concept:** A fictional but plausible unbranded perfume note sheet printed simply on paper: salida / corazón / fondo with ordinary note names. Beside it, a blotter strip. The document should feel like something a reader recognizes, not a polished infographic.

**Composition:** Paper occupies only part of frame; crop some of the list so it feels encountered rather than presented.

**Narrative test:** `This familiar document looks informative enough to be mistaken for a recipe.`

**Clean generation capsule:**
SUBJECT / SCENE: plausible unbranded perfume note sheet beside used blotter strip
ENVIRONMENT: quiet desk/worktable
COMPOSITION: sheet partly cropped, blotter crossing nearby, casual document encounter
CAMERA / POINT OF VIEW: close overhead/oblique
LIGHT: soft natural desk light
MATERIAL / TEXTURE: matte paper, ink, blotter fibers, desk surface
HUMAN IMPERFECTIONS / LIVED-IN DETAILS: slight paper curl, off-angle blotter
PALETTE: paper/ink/mineral neutrals
AUTHENTICITY CONSTRAINTS: generic document; no copied real brand marketing
MUST NOT APPEAR: logos, recognizable branded pyramid, glossy infographic, browser/UI, mockup
CROP / RESPONSIVE INTENT: 4:3 or square master
NARRATIVE TEST: a communication device masquerading as a formula

### Opportunity B — accord explanation

**Decision:** PHYSICAL THREE-TO-ONE COMPOSITION.

**Concept:** Three distinct plausible material references—bergamot peel, labdanum/resin-like material, oakmoss—occupy separate small zones at left/top; at right/bottom, instead of showing a “fourth ingredient,” show one anonymous scent blotter that has received the combined accord. The output cannot be visually reduced to the inputs.

**Composition:** Asymmetric 3→1 flow achieved by spacing and eye movement only; no arrows, labels or diagram lines.

**Narrative test:** `The result exists as an experience, not as a visible sum of ingredient names.`
