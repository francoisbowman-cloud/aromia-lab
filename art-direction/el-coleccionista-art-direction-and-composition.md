## Governing idea

> **Una colección no se ve grande. Se siente como algo que ya dejó de tener un final claro.**

The page must not celebrate abundance as luxury and must not diagnose the reader. Its visual argument follows the article's two opposing anxieties: the pull of what does not yet exist and the fear of losing what already exists.

The dominant formal device is **controlled accumulation**. Objects, repetitions and marginal traces become progressively denser, then the page suddenly recovers air in the `Sí, pero` section. The reader should feel the collection growing almost by accident.

## Atmosphere

Domestic · tactile · slightly obsessive · observant · warm · unspectacular.

This story belongs in a real home, not a fragrance boutique, luxury closet, marble bathroom or influencer shelf. Beauty comes from recognition and composition, not wealth signaling.

## Episodic palette

Foundation remains Aromia warm paper and near-black ink. Story-specific accents:

- paper / bone: `#F2EEE5` family;
- oxidized tobacco-brown: muted, never glossy luxury brown;
- faded bottle-glass green/gray used sparingly;
- one cool steel-gray note for the sections about versions, batches and time;
- no gold-led palette;
- no saturated perfume-ad gradients.

These are perceptual targets, not a requirement to introduce new global design tokens.

## Typographic behavior

Keep Aromia's established editorial family and reading measure. Story-specific gesture: **enumeration without turning into UI**.

Use small running numerals, marginal ticks or understated count marks only where accumulation becomes narratively useful. They should feel like private inventory notation in a notebook, not ecommerce filters, badges, cards or a dashboard.

Avoid oversized pull quotes unless they create a pause. The article's short sentences already provide rhythm.

## Composition principle

The page progresses through four density states:

1. **Recognition** — one lived-in shelf, quiet and close.
2. **Multiplication** — seriality/repetition enters.
3. **Preservation** — older/newer traces, time, backup bottles, version anxiety.
4. **Release** — images disappear and the conclusion returns to plain paper and prose.

The composition must never imply that more bottles equal more status.

## Desktop sequence

### 1. Opening / domestic recognition

Accept the first `[AROMIA_VISUAL_OPPORTUNITY]`.

Preferred opening is not a full-screen glamour hero. Use an **asymmetric editorial opening**: title and dek occupy roughly 48–55% of the field; a tall observational photograph or interpretive domestic still life sits offset into the remaining field.

Image job: show a shelf, drawer or bathroom ledge that has almost run out of room, but in a way that feels inhabited rather than staged. Mixed silhouettes, imperfect spacing, one bottle partly obscured, no rigid symmetry. A few ordinary non-perfume traces are acceptable if they help the scene read as lived-in.

Crop should privilege the behavior of objects occupying space over brand identity. Labels/logos should be unreadable unless the asset is an authentic/licensed documentary photograph with correct identity.

Emotional target: `yo conozco ese estante` rather than `quiero ese estante`.

### 2. Lead / first silence

After the opening image, give the first paragraphs clean paper and generous space. Do not immediately introduce another object sequence. Let the line `Eso ya dice algo.` land alone.

A tiny marginal inventory mark may appear only after the sentence about `¿lo necesito?` no longer being the real question. It should not explain the joke.

### 3. Coleccionar vs acumular

No stock image of a hoarder. No theatrical pile of hundreds of bottles.

Keep this section primarily typographic. If visual support is needed, use a **small documentary-note treatment**: a restrained marginal citation/reference cue connected to the Michelyn Camen material, not a portrait unless an authentic licensable source exists and adds genuine narrative value.

The tension is conceptual. An image would weaken it if it tries to label one behavior as respectable and the other as pathological.

### 4. El objeto tiene la culpa / multiplication field

Density begins here.

Introduce seriality in the page architecture before showing any specific product family: repeated cropped bottle-like silhouettes, inventory ticks or aligned empty positions can progressively occupy a marginal field. These must remain abstract/interpretive and should not mimic real branded packaging.

The device should make the page itself seem to acquire one more object every few paragraphs.

### 5. Le Male flanker passage / factual visual full stop

Accept the second `[AROMIA_VISUAL_OPPORTUNITY]`, but **only with authentic current product identity**.

Preferred composition: a horizontal or gently staggered editorial family study using verified packshots for the actual Jean Paul Gaultier lineup cited in the article. It must read as evidence of multiplication, not a campaign image.

Rules:

- verify current official lineup at implementation time;
- do not generate or reconstruct recognizable Le Male bottles;
- do not invent variants;
- keep packshots on a neutral documentary field;
- use names/year/context as small editorial captions if verified;
- no prices, shop buttons or conversion styling in this passage;
- if rights/source quality is insufficient, replace the family image with a typographic lineage rather than fake visual evidence.

The composition should create a mild perceptual excess: one bottle seems manageable; the family makes the mechanism obvious.

### 6. The transition to scarcity

After the flanker family, reduce density abruptly. A thin rule, a near-empty spread or a generous top gap should signal that the story is switching from `there will always be another` to `what if this one disappears?`.

This silence is important. Do not fill it with smoke, vintage paper textures or nostalgia effects.

### 7. Reformulation / backup bottle / time

Use a **time-based object study**, not a fake Aventus batch infographic.

Preferred visual language: two or three unbranded/identity-neutral bottle positions or photographic details representing `current / reserve / memory`, accompanied by restrained year or batch-like notation that is explicitly interpretive unless based on verified documentary data.

Aventus may appear in text as the article does. If an authentic product image is used, it must not visually claim a particular batch formulation without verified evidence. No fake batch codes, fake vintage labels or claims of olfactory difference encoded as fact.

### 8. Two fears / convergence

This is the compositional peak. Bring both visual systems together:

- one side suggests addition / forward movement;
- the other suggests preservation / holding back.

Do this through spacing and serial notation, not through literal arrows or an infographic. The prose remains dominant.

A compact marginal phrase such as `lo próximo / lo último` may be considered only if Code can keep it semantically decorative and accessible; otherwise omit it.

### 9. Sí, pero / release

All accumulation devices stop.

Return to plain Aromia paper, normal reading column and no image. Let the sentence `A veces uno solo quiere otro perfume.` receive space rather than decoration.

The final paragraphs should feel almost physically easier to read than the middle of the article.

### 10. Commerce close

Affiliate destinations remain contextual footnotes after the editorial ending. No product card row.

Preferred language is explanatory and subordinate, preserving the current article logic: the product is there because it was part of the argument.

If Code uses the existing contextual-commerce primitive, keep it visually quieter than references/captions and clearly disclose affiliation.

## Mobile composition

Mobile should not become a compressed desktop collage.

1. Territory/series marker → title → dek.
2. Opening image full-width but not edge-to-edge if that makes it feel like an ad; retain visible paper margin.
3. First paragraphs with no marginal ornament.
4. Multiplication device becomes **vertical rhythm**: occasional numbered/tick interruptions between paragraphs rather than a side rail.
5. Le Male family becomes a horizontal scroll only if accessible and clearly non-commercial; preferred default is a 2-column/stacked documentary sequence with captions. Never shrink four or five bottles into illegibility.
6. Scarcity transition gets a large vertical gap.
7. Time/preservation visual becomes one simple stacked study.
8. `Sí, pero` removes all visual devices and returns to text.

Mobile image count should be lower than desktop if necessary. Preserve argument, not parity.

## Visual opportunity decisions

### Opportunity 1 — crowded shelf
DECISION: ACCEPT, REINTERPRET.

### Opportunity 2 — Le Male family
DECISION: ACCEPT CONDITIONALLY.

## What not to visualize

- Do not make Michelyn Camen's 600-bottle collection into spectacle.
- Do not visualize `collector vs hoarder` as a binary diagram.
- Do not create fake Aventus vintage/batch evidence.
- Do not use perfume pyramids, molecule graphics or generic ingredient photography.
- Do not add a third branded fragrance just to enrich the page.

## Anti-template test

This page cannot be produced by taking another Aromia article and swapping photographs. Its identity depends on accumulation invading the layout, factual seriality at the Le Male passage, then deliberate visual withdrawal in `Sí, pero`.

If implementation reduces it to `hero image → text → image → text → product cards`, fidelity has failed even if spacing and typography are polished.

## Anti-AI / authored-humanity test

PASS CRITERIA:

- domestic imperfection beats impossible set perfection;
- visual metaphor remains legible without explanatory captions;
- no floating bottles, synthetic fog, liquid splashes, luxury marble or generic amber glow;
- repetition has a narrative reason and stops when the argument stops;
- generated interpretation never masquerades as documentary product evidence;
- the page is willing to leave large stretches without images.

## Accessibility / implementation intent

- visual serial marks are decorative and should not pollute reading order;
- captions identify documentary/official imagery sources when used;
- meaningful images receive specific alt text about what they demonstrate, not marketing adjectives;
- any horizontal product lineage must remain keyboard-accessible or degrade to stacked content;
- reduced-motion mode must not depend on accumulation animation to communicate meaning;
- no necessary fact is conveyed by color alone.

## Motion

Motion is optional, not required.

If used, the only justified behavior is a restrained accumulation reveal as the reader progresses through `El objeto tiene la culpa`. It must remain comprehensible with motion disabled. No parallax bottle theater, magnetic cursors or floating product physics.
