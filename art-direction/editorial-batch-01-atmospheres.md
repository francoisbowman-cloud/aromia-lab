## 01 — El ámbar que nunca tocó una ballena

Territory: **Materia**

### Atmosphere
Mineral · salina · radiante · íntima.

The reader should feel that an invisible familiar smell has suddenly become physical. The visual world is not the ocean and not the whale: it is **matter revealed by light**.

### Visual grammar
- high-key warm-white field;
- translucent/mineral surfaces;
- shallow water, salt-like crystalline behavior, skin-like warmth only as abstraction;
- generous air and fine-scale detail;
- occasional cool mineral gray against Aromia warm paper;
- photography/macro should feel observational rather than cosmetic-advertising.

### Opening composition
Accept the first `[AROMIA_VISUAL_OPPORTUNITY]`, but reinterpret it. Do not show a literal molecular diagram as hero and do not open with whale imagery. Preferred opening: an editorial material study in which translucent/crystalline matter meets a thin film of water and lateral daylight. It should suggest persistence, skin and mineral clarity without claiming to be literal ambroxan crystals unless sourced/verified.

### Pivot composition
Accept the second opportunity. Build a quiet diptych or asymmetric material passage: accurately depicted clary sage / botanical origin against weathered ambergris reference only if a reliable documentary/licensable source is available. If not, omit ambergris rather than fabricate documentary evidence. The contrast is botanical cultivation vs marine scarcity, not good vs bad.

### Product role
Molecule 02 is editorially stronger than Molecule 01 for this story because it makes ambroxan itself visible as concept. Any recognizable bottle must use authentic packshot/identity. Product appears late, after the material/history has earned it.

### Avoid
blue-ocean cliché; whale silhouette; fake lab glassware; fantasy chemistry; luxury-gem ambergris; fragrance-ad hero lighting.

## 02 — El perfumista que no teme exagerar

Territory: **Personas**

### Atmosphere
Exceso controlado · presión · profundidad · precisión dentro de abundancia.

The page should visually perform Ropion's method: something is pushed beyond normal proportion while the surrounding system remains exact.

### Visual grammar
- deep wine / bruised rose / near-black episodic field used selectively, not as global dark mode;
- one visual element allowed to become disproportionate;
- tight crop against disciplined typography;
- repetition that accumulates without becoming decorative floral wallpaper;
- moments of severe restraint after saturation.

### Opening composition
Accept the portrait-adjacent opportunity only with authentic/licensable imagery of Dominique Ropion. Do not generate a fake documentary portrait. If authentic portrait rights are unavailable, open through craft rather than likeness: hands/material blotters/worktable can only be documentary if sourced; otherwise use an interpretive still life and keep the person present through typography/name/verified quote.

### Overdose composition
Strongly accept the second opportunity. This is the signature visual act of the story. Create an interpretive floral composition where recognizable rose material exceeds the expected frame — compressed, layered, almost too much — while a precise empty margin/grid prevents chaos. Patchouli may enter as a dark counter-material, but do not invent botanical hybrids. This is not a product shot.

### Product role
Flowerbomb / La Vie Est Belle / Pure Poison are evidence of range, not a conversion lineup. If shown, authentic isolated packshots may appear as small archival/index-like references rather than a beauty trio. Portrait of a Lady is narratively central but should not be falsely implied as an Aromia affiliate/catalog item unless commercial/source state supports it.

### Avoid
celebrity profile glamour; fake Ropion portrait; roses as generic romance; three-bottle luxury ad; lab infographic; arbitrary percentage graphics.

## 03 — El perfume que encargó un sultán

Territory: **Historias** with series marker **Fuera del radar**

### Atmosphere
Origen · ritual · resina · territorio · encuentro.

The story is not "Arab luxury". It is the unlikely meeting of Omani aromatic history and French perfumery around a commission.

### Visual grammar
- warm limestone / resin amber / smoke charcoal / restrained deep blue;
- hard sun and architectural shadow rather than generic golden luxury glow;
- frankincense smoke used as material/ritual, not mystical fog;
- archival/travel cadence: arrival → territory → materials → bottle/legacy;
- ornament only when derived from real Omani architecture/material culture, never invented pseudo-Arab motifs.

### Opening composition
Accept the first opportunity with a non-literal arrival image. Preferred visual: real Omani material/architecture/trade-landscape reference, or an interpretive composition of limestone light, travel distance and incense material. No fabricated historical portrait of Sultan Qaboos or Guy Robert. No dunes-and-camels shorthand.

### Material-density composition
Accept the second opportunity but do not attempt to illustrate 140 ingredients literally. Build a dense still life from a small number of accurately rendered/sourced key materials named in the text — frankincense, myrrh, cedar and patchouli; ambergris only with truthful representation. Density comes from layering/crop/scale, not fake inventory.

### Product role
Interlude Man and Reflection Man belong in the contemporary legacy/commerce close, not as evidence for the 1983 founding formula. Recognizable bottles require authentic packshots. Gold Woman/Gold Man imagery may be used only if authentic and rights/source conditions are met.

### Avoid
crowns; thrones; gold bars; generic palace fantasy; fake Arabic calligraphy; costume orientalism; invented historical photography; making unverified "most expensive perfume" reputation visually factual.

## Batch-level asset policy

1. Generated imagery is interpretive atmosphere, never documentary evidence.
2. Real people: authentic/licensable portrait or no portrait.
3. Recognizable bottles: authentic packshot/identity only; never generated approximations.
4. Real materials must remain botanically/materially plausible.
5. Captions distinguish interpretation from documentary/source imagery when ambiguity could arise.
6. The moodboard is directional evidence, not a source of truth for product packaging or factual depiction.
