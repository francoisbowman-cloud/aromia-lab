# EL PERFUMISTA QUE NO TEME EXAGERAR — COMPOSITION STUDY

VISUAL_COMPOSITION: READY

## Narrative behavior
The composition performs controlled excess. Density rises until the page almost loses air, then deliberately releases it.

## Desktop sequence
1. **Opening / 80–90vh** — `PERSONAS`, title large but not monumental. If a licensable authentic Ropion portrait exists, use it as an offset vertical image rather than a celebrity full-bleed. Without one, open typographically with an interpretive dark floral field entering from one edge.
2. **Biography/craft** — conventional reading rhythm intentionally calm. Dates and perfume names appear as textual evidence, not cards.
3. **Overdose act** — signature spread. Floral matter expands beyond its column and crop: rose density, dark patchouli counter-material, deep wine episodic background. A precise empty margin keeps the composition authored rather than chaotic.
4. **Works as evidence** — Ysatis / Amarige / Pure Poison / Flowerbomb / Lady Million / La Vie Est Belle appear in a compact typographic chronology. Authentic packshots only if they add a historical reading; never a product carousel.
5. **What he did not use** — abrupt release into near-white paper. Hedione and Iso E Super names occupy small isolated positions with substantial negative space. Absence becomes visible.
6. **Sí, pero** — text remains in the released field; no visual crescendo.
7. **Close** — optional small authentic portrait/craft image if opening did not use one; otherwise finish in text.
8. **Commerce** — restrained links, no trio beauty shot.

## Mobile
Do not reproduce the desktop floral collision literally. Use one dense edge-to-edge floral crop after the introductory paragraphs, then a long clean text release. Chronology becomes a narrow vertical list. The absence section should feel unusually empty even on a phone.

## Image jobs
A. Authentic portrait OR interpretive opening: establish person/craft without celebrity treatment.
B. Interpretive floral excess: embody overdose.
C. Optional authentic historical/product evidence only when editorially useful.

## Restraint
One saturated visual act is enough. Avoid continuous burgundy backgrounds, perfume-ad gloss, fabricated portraits and product lineups.
