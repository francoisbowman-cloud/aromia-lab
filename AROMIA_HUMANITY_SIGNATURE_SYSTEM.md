# Aromia — Humanity Signature System

**Status:** approved visual direction after OMNI-style audit.  
**Use with:** `AROMIA_ART_DIRECTION.md`, `AROMIA_EDITORIAL_DESIGN_LEARNINGS.md`, `AROMIA_EDITORIAL_WORKFLOW.md`.

## Principle

Aromia may use personal, tactile and handwritten elements to strengthen identity, but they must behave as **traces of authorship**, not as decoration added to simulate humanity.

> **La humanidad no se dibuja encima. Se deja ver en las decisiones.**

The objective is not “make it look handmade”. The objective is “make it feel that someone thought, selected, marked, corrected or signed this”.

## Approved identity vocabulary

### 1. Aromia handwritten signature

The handwritten `Aromia` signature is an approved secondary identity asset.

**Role:** closing gesture, intimate brand presence, editorial sign-off.

Use it:
- at the end of selected stories or editions;
- in letters from the editor;
- in editorial notes;
- in occasional cover/edition compositions;
- in contextual brand moments where a sign-off feels natural.

Do not use it:
- as a replacement for the canonical primary wordmark;
- at the end of every card, section or component;
- repeatedly within the same viewport;
- oversized merely for decoration.

The signature should feel discovered, not stamped everywhere.

### 2. `A.` initial

A compact handwritten `A.` may function as a micro-signature.

Possible roles:
- margin-note origin;
- small sign-off;
- footer detail;
- editorial annotation marker;
- quiet acknowledgment of an Aromia observation.

It must remain subordinate to content.

### 3. Marginalia

Short handwritten notes are approved when they carry actual editorial value.

Examples of legitimate functions:
- sensory observation;
- editor's aside;
- cross-reference;
- date/place/reference when relevant;
- a correction or caveat;
- a small invitation to continue reading/exploring;
- a thought that would lose intimacy if converted into a formal subheading.

Rules:
- concise;
- readable;
- never body copy;
- never fake first-person experience;
- never generic filler such as “inspiration”, “memories”, “beautiful details” without specific meaning;
- should not appear on every page.

### 4. Editorial stamps / seals

Allowed only when the mark encodes real information.

Possible uses:
- `HISTORIA`;
- `MATERIA`;
- `PERSONAS`;
- `DESCUBRIMIENTO`;
- edition/issue identifiers;
- archive status;
- field-note status;
- other real editorial classifications.

A seal with no semantic meaning is not part of the system.

### 5. Material traces

Aromia may use tactile evidence such as:
- paper grain;
- real scans;
- ink/pencil marks;
- folds;
- clips;
- handwritten research notes;
- archival labels;
- material swatches;
- physical ephemera related to the story.

These traces should come from real source material when possible. Generated texture must not impersonate evidence.

Do not automatically add:
- coffee stains;
- torn paper;
- masking tape;
- Polaroid frames;
- scrapbook decoration;
- distressed edges;
- fake archival marks.

## Signature asset integrity

Once the final handwritten `Aromia` signature asset is selected, preserve it as a stable brand asset. Do not ask generative systems to redraw it independently on every use, because variation would weaken identity and make the mark visibly synthetic.

The production implementation should eventually use one approved vector/high-resolution asset with controlled variants only where technically necessary.

Until the final source asset is approved, mockups may use the signature concept but must not treat generated approximations as the canonical logo file.

## Frequency rule

Human traces work because they are scarce.

There is **no quota** for handwriting, stamps or texture.

A story can legitimately contain:
- none;
- one signature;
- one meaningful note;
- several editorial traces if the narrative itself is archival/notebook-like.

The same combination must not be repeated automatically from article to article.

## Relationship to article-specific art direction

This system does not define layouts.

The art director decides whether a story benefits from any human trace at all. For example:
- an intimate essay may deserve a closing signature;
- a perfumer profile may use real notebook scans;
- a technical guide may need no handwriting;
- a material story might use field labels or specimen-like marks;
- `El Coleccionista` could use sparse handwritten counting/ownership traces only if they reinforce accumulation rather than decorate it.

> **Repeat relationships, not artifacts.**

## Anti-AI / anti-template gate

Reject the implementation when:

- handwriting appears because the page “needs personality” rather than because the note has something to say;
- the signature becomes a generic footer ornament everywhere;
- several handwriting styles appear without provenance;
- analog imperfection is too clean, too regular or obviously procedural;
- every article uses the same seal/signature/note composition;
- tactile elements compete with reading;
- generated artifacts imply false documentary evidence;
- the page would still feel generic if the handwritten elements were removed.

Human traces must reinforce an already authored composition; they cannot rescue a generic one.

## Actor responsibilities

**Publisher / human**
- may originate real notes, phrases, marks or signature decisions;
- has final authority over canonical signature/identity assets.

**ChatGPT — Art Director**
- decides when human traces improve a story;
- protects scarcity and meaning;
- does not generate fake documentary provenance;
- incorporates the approved signature system into page direction selectively.

**Claude / Cowork — Editorial**
- may propose meaningful marginal notes when they emerge naturally from research or editing;
- must never invent personal memories/experiences to make copy feel human;
- should preserve room for art direction rather than prescribing decorative handwriting.

**Code — Production**
- implements the canonical assets faithfully;
- avoids converting every trace into a mandatory reusable component;
- keeps handwriting accessible where it contains information, including text alternatives or equivalent semantic text when required;
- preserves performance and responsive readability.

**OMNI — Gate**
- checks whether the trace creates authorship, meaning or memory;
- rejects decorative excess, repetition, fake provenance and anti-template regressions.

## Approved direction

The identity system is approved around five concepts:

`SIGNATURE`  
`INITIAL`  
`MEANINGFUL MARGINALIA`  
`SEMANTIC EDITORIAL MARKS`  
`MATERIAL TRACE`

These are a vocabulary, not a checklist.

## Final test

Before adding a humanizing element ask:

> **¿Qué gesto humano real representa esto?**

If the answer is only “hace que se vea más editorial”, do not add it.
