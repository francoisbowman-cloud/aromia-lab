**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Artifact reviewed:** exploratory Aromia identity board generated from the Publisher's request for more human, personalized visual signals.  
**Baseline:** `AROMIA_ART_DIRECTION.md`, `AROMIA_EDITORIAL_DESIGN_LEARNINGS.md`, `GUIA-VISUAL-aromia.md`.

## Audit mode

This is a **repository-grounded OMNI gate**, not a claim that the deployed OMNI runtime was invoked from this chat. The current environment does not expose OMNI as a directly invokable tool. The review applies OMNI's documented evidence-driven closed-loop logic: observe → assess against project design memory → identify blockers → remediate → accept/reject.

## Intent under review

Strengthen Aromia's identity and sense of human authorship through elements such as:

- the handwritten `Aromia` signature;
- marginal notes;
- initials/monograms;
- restrained editorial stamps;
- paper, ink and tactile cues;
- small authorial gestures that make a publication feel directed rather than generated.

The intended outcome is **human presence**, not faux-handmade decoration.

---

## OBSERVE

The exploratory board contains several promising identity cues:

1. A handwritten `Aromia` signature used as a closing gesture.
2. Short handwritten marginal notes and an `A.` initial.
3. Circular editorial stamps/seals.
4. Paper-like texture and subtle material variation.
5. Serif + sans + mono editorial hierarchy.
6. Small editorial numbering and caption-like devices.
7. Natural, quiet palette and object photography.

It also contains risks:

- handwriting appears in many places at once;
- several stamps/monograms coexist;
- paper texture, notes, signature, seals and archival photography can collectively drift into a manufactured “artisan/editorial” aesthetic;
- repeating the same humanizing devices across every page would turn a human trace into another template system.

---

## EXPLAIN

The canonical Aromia direction says the publication should feel **directed, not filled**, should repeat relationships rather than layouts, and should avoid generic visual formulas. The new personalization direction is therefore valuable only when it behaves like evidence of authorship rather than a decorative style layer.

The strongest device is the **signature**, because it has a clear semantic role: closing, approval, presence, authorship. It does not need to explain itself.

Marginal handwriting is also useful when it behaves like an editorial annotation: a thought, observation, date, reference, correction, or small invitation to keep exploring.

The weaker elements are generic seals and simulated imperfections when they are used merely to make something feel “handmade”. A stamp that communicates no editorial state or category is decoration. Fake scribbles that were never part of a real editorial thought are visual theatre.

---

## JUDGE

### ACCEPT

**Aromia handwritten signature**  
Strong identity asset. Human, memorable, compatible with the restrained editorial base. Best used as punctuation rather than as a second primary logo.

**Initial `A.` / compact handwritten mark**  
Useful as a small authorial sign-off, note marker or micro-detail. Keep subordinate to the main wordmark.

**Marginal notes**  
Accepted when they carry actual editorial meaning. They should be short, context-specific and intentionally rare.

**Physical/tactile cues**  
Paper grain, scans, ink marks, folds, clips, pencil/pen traces and archival artifacts are accepted when sourced from real material or deliberately created for the story.

**Editorial numbering / folio logic**  
Accepted because it reinforces publication behavior without pretending to be handmade.

### ACCEPT WITH CONSTRAINTS

**Editorial stamps / seals**  
Allowed only when they encode something real: series, material focus, archive status, field note, discovery marker, editorial edition, etc. Do not use a stamp simply because it looks analog.

**Handwritten text**  
Use as an accent, not as a body-copy system. It must remain readable, semantically justified and sufficiently sparse that it still feels personal.

**Texture**  
Prefer subtle and materially plausible texture. Do not add paper noise to every surface. Pure white remains valid where photography/product fidelity requires it.

### REJECT

- omnipresent handwriting;
- fake personal anecdotes or pseudo-handwritten “human” messages invented to simulate a real person;
- decorative stamps without meaning;
- repeated signature at the end of every card/module;
- fake coffee stains, torn-paper edges, masking tape, Polaroid frames or scrapbook motifs used as automatic “humanity” cues;
- distressed effects that reduce legibility;
- using analog texture to hide weak composition;
- turning the signature into a large hero logo competing with the canonical Aromia wordmark;
- generating a different handwriting style on each page;
- “imperfection” added algorithmically and uniformly.

---

## IMPROVE

The concept is stronger when reframed from **human decoration** to **human trace**.

Aromia should maintain a small vocabulary of traces, but each trace needs a job:

- **Signature:** closure / approval / intimate brand presence.
- **A. initial:** small sign-off or note origin.
- **Margin note:** thought, observation, cross-reference, correction or sensory aside.
- **Folio / number:** orientation and publication rhythm.
- **Stamp:** real editorial classification/state, never decoration alone.
- **Physical mark:** evidence of process/material when relevant to the story.

A publication may use none, one or several depending on the article. There is no requirement that every page display the full vocabulary.

---

## RESTRAIN

The system should stop before it becomes nostalgic cosplay.

Humanity in Aromia must come primarily from:

1. editorial judgment;
2. specific observations;
3. authored composition;
4. factual care;
5. unexpected but purposeful image/text relationships;
6. small traces of process.

The signature cannot compensate for generic layouts. A handwritten note cannot make generic AI copy human. Texture cannot make a fake bottle authentic.

---

## Gate result

**PASS WITH CONSTRAINTS**

The personalization direction materially strengthens Aromia **if implemented as a sparse semantic system rather than a decorative kit**.

### Approved core

`SIGNATURE + INITIAL + MEANINGFUL MARGINALIA + REAL EDITORIAL MARKS + MATERIAL TRACE`

### Blocking condition for future use

If the same set of signature + note + stamp + texture appears mechanically across multiple unrelated articles, the direction fails the anti-template gate and must be reduced.
